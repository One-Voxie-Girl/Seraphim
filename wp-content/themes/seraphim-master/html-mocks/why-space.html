Why now?

The economics have fundamentally shifted

In recent years, entrepreneurs have been taking technology components from consumer electronics to create satellites that are smaller, faster and cheaper than ever before. These satellites are now the size of a microwave or a shoebox but with the functionality of the satellites that once cost hundreds of millions of dollars.

Rockets can now be refurbished and reused, cutting launch costs by over 90%. This means it is now possible to launch hundreds or even thousands of satellites at a time. This is the build-out of what we call the digital infrastructure in the sky.

Why it matters

Space, solving humanity's most pressing problems

Satellites provide essential data that helps us monitor our planet. They support climate resilience, national security, food systems, AI workflows, energy grids, aviation, and atmospheric change.

Climate & sustainability

Persistent Earth observation can support emissions monitoring, climate-risk modelling, and disaster response.
Defence & security

Space infrastructure provides resilient communications, surveillance, and navigation for sovereign capability.
Artificial intelligence

Orbital data is becoming a critical input for AI models that understand activity across the physical world.
Food security

Satellite analytics help farmers track crop health, water stress, weather risk, and supply-chain disruption.
